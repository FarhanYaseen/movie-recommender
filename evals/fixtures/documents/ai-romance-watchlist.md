# Watchlist: machines, memory, and love

A short annotated watchlist about intimacy with the artificial and the
fragility of memory, kept as viewing notes.

## Her (2013)

Spike Jonze's Her is set in a soft-focus near-future Los Angeles where
Theodore, a professional letter-writer, falls for Samantha, an operating
system with a warm voice and no body. The film refuses easy irony: the
relationship is treated as real, including its jealousy and its growing
asymmetry as Samantha upgrades beyond human tempo. The breakup scene — she is
simultaneously in love with hundreds of others — lands like an honest account
of incompatible scales rather than a twist.

## Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004)

Michel Gondry's film imagines Lacuna, a clinic that deletes painful memories.
Joel, mid-procedure, changes his mind and starts hiding Clementine inside
unrelated memories while the erasure crew hunts them down. The collapsing
beach house is the image that stays: a relationship's spaces dissolving as the
deletion progresses. The film's bittersweet answer is that the couple, warned
by tape recordings of their past selves, chooses to try again anyway.

## Arrival (2016)

Denis Villeneuve's Arrival belongs here for its grammar of grief. Linguist
Louise Banks learns the heptapods' circular written language and with it a
nonlinear experience of time, perceiving her daughter's future life and death
before choosing it. The film's structural trick — what look like flashbacks
are flash-forwards — turns a first-contact procedural into a meditation on
consenting to loss.
